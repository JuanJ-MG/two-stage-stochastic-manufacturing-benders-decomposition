This project studies a two-year manufacturing planning problem motivated by aircraft spare-parts supply chains. In the first year, a manufacturer must decide how many machines to purchase—including additive manufacturing (AM) and traditional manufacturing (TM) technologies—before future demand is known. These are expensive, long-term decisions that cannot be easily reversed. In the second year, after demand is realized, the system responds by deciding how much to produce, how much labor and overtime to use, and whether to add short-term capacity, while incurring penalties if demand cannot be met.

Demand in Year 2 is uncertain and represented using multiple scenarios with associated probabilities. The goal is to minimize expected total cost across both years, including machine investment, operating and labor costs, and unmet-demand penalties, while respecting budget and labor constraints.

To solve this problem efficiently, the model is implemented using Benders decomposition, which separates the Year-1 investment decisions from the Year-2 operational planning problems and iteratively coordinates them until an optimal solution is found. This approach makes it possible to solve the problem with multiple demand scenarios while keeping the long-term investment decisions integer-valued.

Although motivated by aircraft manufacturing and supply chains, the formulation and solution approach are broadly applicable to logistics and infrastructure planning problems under uncertainty, demonstrating the use of MILP-based stochastic optimization and decomposition techniques for structured decision-making in complex, resource-constrained systems.
